    Example answers

    Which email platform do you send from?

    Your list uses the menus for your platform. "Other" covers tools like Brevo, ActiveCampaign or Kit; the steps are the same idea.

    What address do your emails come from?

    Used only on this page, to fill in the records below. Nothing is sent anywhere.

    Gmail, Yahoo and Microsoft check that bulk email really comes from the domain in the "From" line. A free mailbox can't pass that check.

    Is your domain set up to prove your email is really from you?
    SPFLists the services allowed to send as you
    DKIMA digital signature on every email
    DMARCTells inboxes what to do if the first two fail

    These are three small text records at whoever hosts your domain (GoDaddy, Cloudflare, Squarespace, Namecheap and so on). Your platform's domain settings page usually shows a green tick for each. Since 2024, Gmail and Yahoo require all three from anyone sending 5,000 or more emails a day to their users.

    How big is your list, and when did it last get an email?

    A list that has gone quiet for months is where most spam traps and dead addresses hide.

    Do you know your bounce and spam complaint rates?

    A bounce is an email that couldn't be delivered. A spam complaint is someone clicking "Report spam". Your campaign reports show both.

    Where are your subscribers? (tick all that apply)

    Each country has its own email law. The strictest one that applies to a subscriber is the one to follow.

            How we calculate this

            An inbox risk is anything that can send your email to spam or break an email law: sending from a free mailbox, missing SPF, DKIM or DMARC, a bought list, a list not mailed in six months or more, no unsubscribe link, no postal address, known high bounce or complaint rates, or imported EU/UK contacts with no record of consent. Answers of "not sure" become checks at the top of your list, not risks, until you know.

            The list order follows the work: your sending address first, then the domain records, then the list itself, then the rules. Times are rough first-time ranges, not promises. DNS changes can take up to 48 hours to show; that wait is extra.

            Gmail and Yahoo's bulk-sender rules (in force since February 2024) apply to anyone sending about 5,000 or more emails a day to their users; Microsoft added similar rules for Outlook.com in 2025. We flag them when your list is 5,000 or more. The rate limits quoted (spam complaints under 0.1%, never 0.3% or more) come from Google's sender guidelines.
