How Do You Go to Market?

List where your marketing money goes today, then walk through how customers find you and decide. You get a snapshot of your spend against the buyer journey, the places they don't line up, and a first draft of the message every channel should carry. Everything is built from your answers, and you can edit all of it.

Strategy starters · optional

Each one stands on its own. Do them in any order, or skip straight to a toolkit.

    How you go to market

    Example answers

    Where your marketing money goes

    Tick every place you spend money on marketing today, including agencies and software. Add a rough monthly amount and whether it's working. Your ad platforms, your accounting software or a credit card statement have the numbers.

    Your customer journey

    How buyers get from never having heard of you to buying, and what happens next. Free ways to check:

    Your message

    Finish these sentences so every channel above says the same thing. You get a positioning statement, a promise and lines for each channel.

    What you are, in a few words: “bookkeeping firm”, “roofing company”, “project management app”.

    Be specific. The customers you'd clone if you could: who they are and what situation they're in.

    The result behind the purchase, not the product: “to know where every dollar goes”, or “peace of mind”. Nobody wants a bookkeeper; they want to know where the money goes.

    What does your industry get wrong, or overlook? This is the belief that sets you apart. It's about how the industry works, not about one competitor.

    What you actually do differently because of that belief. Something a competitor couldn't say without changing how they work.

    Finish it in your customer's words, about them. If a customer has said it in a review or an email, use their phrase.

    One per line. These are your proof: the habits that make the promise true.

    A type of competitor, or doing it themselves, or doing nothing. Not a company name.

    We sound… (pick up to three)

    How you'd describe your voice to a new writer. The “never” is as useful as the rest.

    Your go-to-market, in one line

    $0a month on marketing

    Your own snapshot. Built from your answers and rough numbers, not from your books or ad accounts. Use it to decide where to look closer.

    Your spend against the buyer journey

    Where your money goes at each stage, next to where you said buyers actually are. Rough splits: some channels work at more than one stage.

    Where it doesn't line up

                What this is, and what it isn't

                • It is a working draft, built only from your answers, to get consistent words onto your site, ads and emails now.
                • It isn't research or an audit of your ad accounts. The spend and stage splits are rough, from your answers. Test the message: read your positioning to three customers and ask what they'd change.
                • It isn't a full brand strategy. That goes deeper into what you believe and why, every audience you serve, your story and your voice, and it takes a strategist working with your team.
                • Nothing here changes your other toolkits. Copy the lines you like into them yourself.

                Use it, then test it

                Copy or print your go-to-market snapshot and messaging starter. Start with the first thing that doesn't line up. Put the positioning on your homepage and the promise in your next email, and listen to what customers say back. If you want to go deeper, a strategist can help; that's optional.
