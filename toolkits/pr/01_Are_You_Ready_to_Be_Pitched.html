    Who will talk to reporters?

    A spokesperson is the one person quoted by name. Reporters want a real person, not "the company".

    How fast can they reply to a reporter and take an interview?

    Rule of thumb: many reporter requests close within a day or two. A late reply usually means a missed story.

    Who has to approve a quote before it goes out?

    The approvals path is who signs off and how fast. Slow sign-off stalls a program as surely as no spokesperson.

    Are 2–3 expertise topics written down?

    The subjects your spokesperson can speak on with real experience, for example "home battery backup", not "energy".

    What could be news in the next 90 days? Pick any

    Leave all blank if nothing comes to mind yet. That is common, and the plan shows how to find one.

            How we count gaps

            The checklist has ten items. Nine of them can stall a program in month one: without them, a pitch can't go out, a reporter can't get a reply in time, or a story can't run. Those are what the headline counts.

            The tenth, a list of past coverage, is polish. It helps credibility but never blocks a first pitch, so it shows as a gap in the list without adding to the headline.

            "Someone in mind", "within a week", "a few people, a few days" and "ideas only" count as gaps. They're close, but they're the answers that most often cost a story. No numbers here are measured from your accounts.

            Free tools and plan limits change. Check your own account before you build. Earned media is never guaranteed; this checklist gets you ready to be pitched, not placed.
